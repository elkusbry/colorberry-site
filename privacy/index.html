Colorberry Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 9, 2026

Colorberry is a coloring app for families. This policy explains what happens to information when you use it. Short version: we (the developer) don't run any servers, don't have accounts, don't show ads and don't track you.

What we collect

We don't collect or see any of your data. Colorberry has no analytics, no advertising, no third-party tracking SDKs and no developer-run server.

Making a new picture (Google Gemini)

"Make a new picture" is for grown-ups only: it opens behind a parental gate, and only works after a grown-up enters their own Google Gemini API key and agrees in a consent prompt that names Google Gemini. When a grown-up makes a picture, the app sends the picture idea (the words typed or spoken, for example "a cat in a rocket") to Google's Gemini API, together with fixed drawing instructions, so Gemini can draw a coloring page. That's the only thing sent: no name, no account details, no device identifiers, no photos or drawings.

  • The request goes straight from the device to Google under the grown-up's own API key. We never receive it.
  • Google handles it under the terms and privacy policy that apply to that key (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms and https://policies.google.com/privacy). Depending on the Google account and billing setup, Google may keep or use prompts as described in those terms.
  • The API key is stored in the device's Keychain and isn't shared with us.
  • If no key is entered, nothing is sent to Google.

Speaking a picture idea

The microphone button uses Apple's speech recognition to turn speech into text. The app asks for on-device recognition when the device supports it; otherwise Apple may process the audio under Apple's privacy policy. The app doesn't record or keep audio.

Drawings, kids' names and family sharing (iCloud)

Kids' nicknames, picture avatars and finished drawings are stored on the device and, if iCloud is on, in the family's own private iCloud (CloudKit) storage. Apple stores this data; we as the developer can't access it. It's shared only with family devices and with friends' families that a grown-up invites, and a grown-up can stop any share or remove a kid at any time, which deletes that kid's data everywhere it was shared.

"Save to Photos" saves a copy of a finished picture to the device's Photos library. Those copies stay in Photos if a drawing is later deleted in the app.

Settings on the device

The daily picture limit and a count of pictures made (to estimate Gemini costs) are kept on the device only.

Children

Colorberry is meant to be set up by a parent or guardian. Making new pictures with Google Gemini is a grown-ups-only feature behind a parental gate, so children don't send anything to Google. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children.

Deleting your data

Delete drawings or kids inside the app, or delete the app. To remove iCloud copies, go to Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Manage Storage > Colorberry.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date above and the policy in the app's App Store listing.
